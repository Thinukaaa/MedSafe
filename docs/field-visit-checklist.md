# Field visit checklist — pharmacy (tonight)

Ask permission first. Do not photograph customers, prescriptions or patient names.

## Photos to take
- [ ] Shop front (name visible, if the owner agrees)
- [ ] Medicine shelves (shows how much stock there is to watch)
- [ ] Close-up of 3–5 box labels showing **batch no. + expiry date + price**
- [ ] The expired / return box or bag (if they have one)
- [ ] How they track expiry now (notebook, stickers, nothing?)
- [ ] You + the pharmacist (stakeholder evidence), if they agree

## Questions (write the answers word for word)
1. Your name and position? How long have you worked here?
2. How do you check for expiring medicines now? How often?
3. In the last 3 months, roughly how much stock did you throw away as expired? (Rs or number of boxes)
4. Will suppliers take back near-expiry stock? How many days/months before expiry?
   → this sets the **warning window** in Settings
5. Which items expire most often? (slow-moving items, syrups, fridge items?)
6. Has anyone ever nearly sold an expired item? (safety angle)
7. Who would use an app like this — you, an assistant? On a phone or PC?
8. Would you pay Rs ___ per month for it? What would make it worth it?

## Data to enter into the app (real, not sample)
Enter 5–10 real batches from the shelf: name, strength, batch no., expiry, qty, cost price.
Source = **Box label** or **Pharmacist told us (field visit)**.

## For the business component
- Expired loss per month (from Q3) × 12 = yearly loss  → cite: stakeholder interview
- Share caught in time = **ASSUMPTION** (state it, e.g. use the app's recovery rate)
- Price per month (from Q8) → payback = yearly saving ÷ yearly price
